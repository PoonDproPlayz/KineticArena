## Goal and user requirements

Build a 500 g combat robot practice simulator with MuJoCo physics. Import a Fusion 360 robot as individual bodies, retain the Fusion occurrence/folder tree and available material, appearance, and mass information, and let the user assign one or more bodies to a main frame, each wheel, or a weapon. For example, the weapon's frame, blades, and screws must share one rigid weapon assembly and one rotation axis. The user needs a manual weight for every part and editable physical settings.

The arena and fighting robot remain the main 3D view. The native GUI now opens to a Home screen and has persistent Home, Drive, and Bot workshop navigation. The workshop presents assembly controls beside a separate, selectable 3D robot preview. Users select parts from that preview and choose an axis target from a weapon/wheel/fork dropdown. Hover highlighting exists, and there is an attempted closed-loop detector for polygonal holes, but reliable Prusa-style rim hover and one-click axis selection is **not complete**: the current Sparrow bundle test failed to find a selectable rim. They can hide/show CAD bodies independently of physics inclusion, plus orbit, tilt, pan, and zoom. The Drive screen includes pause/reset, editable target speeds, and actual joint-speed telemetry. A simple one-command setup and separate everyday launcher are available.

## Current standalone implementation

- `Standalone/main.cpp` is a native C++ app using MuJoCo 3.3.7, its classic OpenGL renderer, SDL3, and Dear ImGui. Filament was discussed as the intended rendering upgrade, but is **not implemented** and requires a MuJoCo upgrade. Do not describe the current app as Filament based.
- The stock model is `Assets/CombatSim/Models/CombatRobot500g.xml`. It has a 0.0004 s (2,500 Hz) physics timestep, a 2 m arena, two practice robots, and a 30,000 RPM player weapon target. W/S drive, A/D turn, Space spins the weapon; at zero throttle A/D counter-rotate the two wheel sides. Configured wheel motors set the full-throttle target from `Kv x battery voltage / gear ratio`; a checkbox enables a 0–5,000 RPM speed-servo override. The gamepad uses the left stick and left shoulder or south button. The Drive screen reports live actual weapon and per-wheel RPM, side averages, and wheel actuator counts.
- The app opens at Home; the top navigation switches between Home, Drive, and Bot workshop. Home and workshop pause physics; Drive runs it unless paused. The arena remains rendered as the background. Bot workshop places the Fusion tree and editor in a scrollable left pane with the distinct selectable CAD preview beside it. The CAD preview uses its own fixed MuJoCo model, `combatsim_preview.xml`, while `combatsim_generated.xml` is the simulated assembly model.
- Drive defaults to a motor-derived no-load speed estimate for each side and lets the motor torque/contact simulation determine loaded RPM. **Manual wheel RPM override** switches to a speed servo that bypasses wheel motor torque for forced-speed checks. If no weapon motor torque model is enabled, legacy weapon target RPM and spin-up controls are editable; motor mode instead shows a modeled 0-to-90% spin-up estimate. Live telemetry reads actual hinge velocity from each actuator's joint transmission and converts rad/s to RPM. Weapon RPM is shown as magnitude; each wheel is shown signed in the normalized forward direction, with left/right averages. The session starts paused at Home.
- The Drive dashboard sidebar can be widened by dragging its right edge. Its controls are in a clipped, vertically scrollable child region; long help/status text wraps within the panel instead of spilling over the arena.
- Drive telemetry reports each assigned wheel as **GRIP**, **SLIPPING**, **AIRBORNE**, or **SLIP UNKNOWN**. It computes wheel and floor velocities at each MuJoCo contact point with contact Jacobians, removes normal motion, and flags tangential tread speed above 0.18 m/s. This detects contact-patch sliding directly and does not infer slip from commanded RPM.
- The arena remains at 2,500 Hz. To reduce high-speed wall penetration, the four transparent side walls now have 10 cm collision thickness while keeping their inner faces at the same 2 m arena boundary, with a 1 cm contact margin and a stiffer wall-specific contact response. Stock and Fusion-generated arena XML use the same settings. A stock-model impact test compares 2,500 Hz and 10,000 Hz at 24 m/s and 100 m/s; the added wall margin/contact tuning keeps the robot within the tested envelope without increasing normal simulation cost.
- `Standalone/FusionBundle.cpp/.h` loads schema 2 Fusion bundles and older schema 1 bundles, checks all referenced STLs before import, and exposes the occurrence tree. Bodies and folders can be assigned to Main frame, wheel or weapon rigid assemblies, or Exclude. Multiple bodies can belong to one moving assembly; additional assemblies can be added.
- The CAD editor now has a **Gravity-driven fork modules** count. Each fork is its own `GravityFork` assembly so the user can assign its CAD bodies independently, set its hinge pivot/axis, and edit travel limits in degrees (default +/-90 degrees around the imported pose). Fork hinges have no actuator; their mass distribution responds to gravity. Each fork has a **Collide with main frame** checkbox, persisted as `forkCollidesWithFrame`; enabled modules get explicit fork-geom/main-frame-geom pairs in generated MJCF. Reducing the count only removes empty modules; assigned modules must be reassigned first. The assembly tree ID is stable while its visible name is edited, so name typing retains focus. Fork axis markers are purple.
- Per-body **Weight (g)** accepts a manual mass; **Use Fusion** restores the exported mass. The importer also exposes color and friction overrides and displays physical material data. `combatsim_setup.json` next to the manifest saves assignments, pivots, axes, overrides, and preview visibility. Saved masses use kg internally. For a multi-body rotating assembly, each assigned body's Fusion mass and transformed COM are combined with a mass-weighted average. No balancing projection is applied. The UI displays the combined assembly COM's radial offset from the selected shaft, so changing the axis changes the simulated imbalance. The exporter now includes each body's centroidal inertia tensor when Fusion provides principal moments and axes; the importer rotates and mass-scales each tensor, then combines them at the group COM with the parallel-axis theorem and writes MuJoCo `fullinertia`. Manual mass scaling assumes the within-body mass distribution stays similar; model added material as another body when its location matters. Legacy bundles and parts without Fusion inertia retain the bounding-box estimate. Older `assumeBalanced` values in setup JSON are ignored. Fusion material data is retained for review, but texture/elasticity data is not mapped to MuJoCo and contact friction is an editable estimate.
- The CAD preview supports part clicking, selection highlight, **Assign selected part**, **Deselect part**, part/folder visibility checkboxes and Show/Hide controls, plus **Show only selected**. Preview visibility is independent of physical inclusion and persists as `partOverrides.previewVisible`. Hidden parts are also filtered from picking. Left drag orbits/tilts, right drag pans, and scroll zooms; the canvas captures dragging without moving the editor pane. Reset view and a tilt control are available.
- The **Axis target (weapon / wheel / fork)** dropdown above the 3D preview selects the intended moving assembly and persists across reloads. Folder assignment controls display the current assignment; assigning a folder or part to a rotating assembly also selects it as the axis target and exits stale axis-pick mode. Axis picking suppresses selected-body and hovered-body color overlays, filters hover to visible parts assigned to the chosen rotating assembly, and offsets the cyan rim loop toward the preview camera to avoid surface z-fighting. Closed-loop detection sweeps several crease angles to find chamfered or tessellated bore rims, deduplicates loops, and uses a size-relative hover tolerance. The hover message reports when no closed loop was found or when the cursor is away from a rim. Synthetic tests cover mass-weighted COM across two weapon bodies, chamfered-ring hover and fit, rejecting a body outside the target assembly, and saved assignment/axis persistence.

## Export, run, and import

- Fusion exporter: `Fusion360Exporter/CombatSimExport.py` with `config.json`. In Fusion 360, open **Utilities > Add-ins > Scripts and Add-ins**, add the `Fusion360Exporter` folder, and run `CombatSimExport` on the open design. It writes `<design>_MuJoCo/manifest.json` and binary STLs in nested `Parts` folders matching occurrences. Schema 2 includes native transforms, per-body mass/density/material properties, centroidal inertia tensors when available, and appearance color/roughness, while preserving fields used by the older Unity importer. A fallback B-Rep tessellation path handles cases where Fusion reports STL export success without writing a valid file; each STL is validated before its manifest entry is committed. Exporting all relevant bodies, including hidden ones, is part of the workflow; verify actual Fusion behavior if changing this.
- First-time Windows setup from repo root: `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\Standalone\setup.ps1`. It installs missing Git, CMake, and Visual Studio 2022 C++ Build Tools with WinGet, downloads MuJoCo 3.3.7 into ignored `Standalone/.deps`, builds, and launches. ## Motor torque work (2026-10-20)

The first motor torque implementation covered wheel drive assemblies only. In **Drive motor settings**, wheel assemblies can be assigned to motor groups A-H. Assemblies in one group share one motor specification and its total torque is divided evenly across the member wheel assemblies. Configure battery voltage and group motor count, Kv (RPM/V), peak current per motor, and optional phase-to-phase winding resistance and ESC phase-current limit. Gear ratio and efficiency are editable. The model derives Kt from Kv and uses voltage/back-EMF/current to estimate torque; without resistance, it uses a current-limited linear torque/speed approximation. Peak current is treated as torque-producing motor current; motor heating and thermal derating are not implemented. A datasheet value that describes only battery input current is approximate. Older `ratedCurrentA` setup values still load. Grouped wheels still rotate independently; chain/belt/differential speed coupling is not modeled. The weapon torque model and colon gear-ratio input were added later; see the latest section below.

Validation at that stage: `--test-motor-torque` exercises both resistance and fallback curves plus conservation of total torque across two wheel assemblies; `--test-motor-actuator <manifest>` runs a synthetic shared-motor group under MuJoCo. The synthetic one-second spin check reported 4,664 RPM against a 5,550 RPM no-load speed. This checked wiring and stability, not physical calibration.

## Motor-derived wheel RPM and manual override (2026-10-21)

Automatic Drive mode now uses each side's slowest configured motor no-load output speed, `Kv x battery voltage / gear ratio`, as its full-throttle target. The torque actuator uses the requested speed, back-EMF, current limit, gear ratio, and efficiency; the resulting loaded wheel RPM comes from simulated motor torque, wheel inertia, and contacts. If a side has no configured motor, it retains the 424 RPM legacy fallback and the UI identifies the missing model. Drive shows the no-load estimate separately from live actual RPM.

The **Manual wheel RPM override** checkbox enables an independent velocity servo with a 0–5,000 RPM target (initialized to 4,000 RPM). This deliberately bypasses wheel motor torque so a user can force a speed for checks. Motor-enabled joints have a second velocity actuator for this mode; its target is held at measured joint speed while automatic torque mode is active, so it does not brake the motor.

## USB joystick mixer (2026-10-21)

The Home quick-start area has a **Controller mixing** button, and the Drive screen has a **Controller mixer** button. Both open the same modal setup; the separate Drive Mixing tab was removed. Raw SDL USB joysticks include flight sticks/controllers that SDL does not identify as a standard gamepad. Select a device by its GUID; device add/remove events refresh the active handle. Up to 16 input channels can bind to joystick axes or buttons, with axis reverse/deadzone controls and live meters. Select a physical drive motor group or Weapon, then edit each channel mix line's weight, offset, and expo. Drive outputs clamp to -100..100%; Weapon clamps to 0..100%. The response graph samples the selected input channel while holding other live channels at their current values; its dot marks the real controller input/output and moves as the joystick changes. Default RC/elevon-style mix is Ch1-Ch2 for left drive, Ch1+Ch2 for right drive, and Ch3 for Weapon. Custom joystick mode is opt-in; keyboard and standard gamepad controls remain the fallback when no raw joystick is connected. Mixer device/channel/output settings plus weights, offsets, and expo auto-save on edits and at app exit to SDL's per-user app preferences as `input_mixer.json`, separate from each bot's Fusion setup and retained across bot loads.
